Pure sine wave vs modified sine wave

Grid electricity is a smooth "sine wave". A pure sine wave inverter reproduces it; a modified (square) wave inverter approximates it with crude steps. The difference matters more than the price tag.

What runs fine on modified sine wave

  • Incandescent/LED lights, fans, phone chargers, resistive heaters.

What needs pure sine wave

  • Anything with a motor or compressor: fridges, freezers, water pumps, ACs — modified wave makes them run hot, buzz and can shorten their life.
  • Electronics with sensitive power supplies: TVs, computers, WiFi routers.
  • Medical equipment and some UPS/AVR units.

The false economy

A modified wave inverter is cheaper to buy, but if it damages a Rs. 60,000 fridge you have paid many times over. For a whole home we only stock pure sine wave — for example the Simtek MPPT controller pairs with pure sine wave inverters for a clean system.

Quick rule

If the load is only lights and fans on a tight budget, modified wave is acceptable. The moment a fridge, TV or computer is on the backup, buy pure sine wave.
